\begin{abstract}
We study real-world exploration under an intentionally weak sensing budget: no camera input to the policy, no LiDAR, no SLAM, and no reconstructed map. A small rover must choose local actions from only onboard range, inertial, action-history, and executor-feedback signals. The proposed direction, Tiny World Feedback (TWF), replaces visual-memory exploration with a compact sensory encoder, recurrent state, and SAC local-heading policy. The central claim is not that vision is unnecessary in general, but that useful exploration behavior can emerge from auditable world feedback when the policy is forced to rely on what the robot physically experienced. This paper is being rewritten around the resulting question: how far can a tiny visionless rover explore before mapping or perception-heavy representations become necessary?
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autonomous exploration is usually formulated as mapping: infer free space, build a representation, and plan toward frontiers \citep{yamauchi1997frontier,thrun2005probabilistic}. We instead target a deliberately smaller regime. The robot has cheap range sensors, motion feedback, and motor execution reports, but no map and no visual observation in the policy. This constraint matches small low-cost robots where cameras are unreliable, compute is scarce, and the most trustworthy supervision is the world feedback produced by attempted motion.

The practical problem is that naive intrinsic motivation can reward loops. A rover circling in a kitchen may keep moving safely and still fail to discover anything. Our objective is therefore to make exploration depend on grounded progress: executed motion, change in recent sensory state, recovery events, near-obstacle readings, and loop penalties. Every reward component is logged so policy behavior can be audited after real runs.

\section{Method: Tiny World Feedback}

At step $t$, the policy observes
\begin{equation}
o_t = [d_t, m_t, a_{t-1}, e_{t-1}],
\end{equation}
where $d_t$ are range readings, $m_t$ is a compact motion summary, $a_{t-1}$ is the previous high-level action, and $e_{t-1}$ is executor feedback such as distance achieved, turn duration, zero-progress flag, and recovery flag. There is no image term.

The encoder is intentionally tiny. A small MLP embeds the current sensory vector and a GRU maintains short-term context. SAC then outputs a scalar relative heading. The executor turns toward that heading and drives until a front-range threshold, timeout, contact/stall signal, or recovery event terminates the action.

\section{Reward}

The reward is designed to make loops unprofitable:
\begin{align}
r_t ={}& w_n N_t + w_d \Delta s_t + w_s S_t \\
&- \lambda_z Z_t - \lambda_r R_t - \lambda_o O_t - \lambda_l L_t.
\end{align}
Here $N_t$ is sensory novelty over recent compact states, $\Delta s_t$ is executed distance, and $S_t$ is safe-motion success. Penalties represent zero progress, recovery, near-obstacle operation, and repeated loop patterns. Distance reward is not sufficient by itself: repeated movement through the same sensory state should be penalized even if the rover is not stuck.

\section{Offline DINO Analysis Only}

Frozen DINO image clustering remains useful as analysis. We keep an offline script that clusters saved images and can produce a paper figure showing that visual foundation features separate room-like observations. This is not part of TWF inference. A possible future ablation may use DINO cluster novelty as an external reward signal, but DINO features must still be excluded from the GRU/SAC observation.

\section{Planned Experiments}

The immediate experiments compare reward variants on the real rover:

\begin{itemize}
  \item TWF with distance-only progress reward.
  \item TWF with sensory novelty and revisit penalty.
  \item TWF with stronger loop penalty and distance reward gated by novelty.
  \item Optional offline DINO reward-only ablation, with no visual policy input.
\end{itemize}

Metrics are scalar return, executed distance, zero-progress fraction, recovery count, loop count, and human-labeled exploration quality from run video or direct observation.

\section{Conclusion}

This draft now focuses on tiny mapless exploration from world feedback. The old visual-memory model is removed from the runtime system. The remaining question is sharper: can a very small recurrent policy using only low-cost sensors learn to avoid loops and discover new parts of an environment on real hardware?
